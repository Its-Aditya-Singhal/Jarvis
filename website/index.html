LEVEL0

It keeps asking who's there.

L0

Nobody, or somebody else

An unknown face, a photo held up to the camera, or a frozen video feed: nothing is unlocked and the attempt is written to the security log. No scores are shown to whoever is sitting there.

L1 · READ

Your face, and you're alive

ArcFace matches your face over several frames. A passive anti-spoof model and random challenges (blink, turn, lean in) prove it's a live person. Now it can answer questions and read your calendar.

L2 · ACT

Plus your voice, in the last minute

An ECAPA voiceprint confirms it's you speaking, with nobody else in view. Now it can set alarms, open apps, write notes and control the Mac.

L3 · CONFIRM

Deleting anything needs a yes

A fresh liveness check and an explicit "yes, go ahead" in your verified voice, or a click, within 30 seconds. A fusion model weighs all the evidence, and it can only ever lower a level.

Everyday things, said out loud.

Alarms and timers

"Wake me at 7 and note to buy milk" becomes two actions. Snooze or just say "stop".

Calendar and notes

Stored encrypted in the app, with optional sync to Apple Calendar and Notes.

Your Mac, by voice

Open and quit apps, volume, music, brightness, dark mode, screenshots, lock the screen.

Files

"The PDF I downloaded yesterday": found, shown in Finder, or moved to the Trash after you confirm.

Memory

"Remember that my sister's birthday is 12 March." Ask later in any of the three languages.

Instant answers

Math, unit conversions, the time and common commands skip the language model entirely.

Ask Claude or ChatGPT

Dictate a prompt; it opens a new chat with the prompt written in and waits for you to send it.

Your name for it

Call it JARVIS, FRIDAY or anything else. The name you pick is also the wake word.

What stays, and what never exists.

Biometrics can't be changed if they leak, so they never leave the Mac and are never stored as pictures or recordings.

Kept, encrypted

  • Face and voice embeddings (numbers, not images or audio)
  • Notes, alarms, events, memory and chat history
  • A security log of blocked attempts

Never stored

  • Camera frames or photos
  • Audio recordings
  • Speech that wasn't addressed to the assistant

Offline by default

The backend refuses every connection except to itself. The Privacy view lists anything that tried.

Local-only API

Bound to 127.0.0.1 with a fresh random token each launch, and DNS-rebinding checks.

You can see and delete it

A privacy dashboard shows every kind of data with its size and age. Export or erase any of it.

Honest about limits

A replayed voice recording can pass voice checks, which is why actions also need your live face. Read the threat model.

Get JARVIS 1.0

macOS 14 Sonoma or later on Apple Silicon (M1 or newer), 16 GB of memory recommended and about 10 GB of free space for the models.

Download the .dmg
  1. Open the .dmg and drag JARVIS to Applications.
  2. The app isn't notarized by Apple. The first time, open it, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and press Open Anyway.
  3. On first launch it downloads its models (about 1.7 GB) and helps you install Ollama for the language model (about 5 GB more).

Checksums are published with every release. Full user guide.
